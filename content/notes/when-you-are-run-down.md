When your body is in poor condition, when you feel weak or run down, the thing to protect is not sexual activity but recovery capacity. Your reserves are already spent. Anything that adds load — including sexual activity — is competing with the work your body needs to do to get back to baseline.

The most common version of this advice arrives wrapped in a claim about energy and depletion, and that claim does not survive contact with the evidence. There is no established physiological mechanism by which ejaculation drains energy that a body needs for healing, and no trial showing that avoiding it speeds recovery from illness. It is traditional medicine, not urology, and it also runs against what the rest of this site argues: regular release is generally good for the prostate and the pelvic floor, and a healthy pattern is one where congestion resolves and the floor relaxes.

So the advice worth keeping is the recovery advice. Rest properly and get enough sleep. Do light, moderate exercise rather than nothing and rather than too much. Keep up the baseline level of physical activity your body needs to function. Keep your emotions stable and your head calm.

That is not a small thing. Most men in a bad patch do the opposite. They stop moving entirely, sleep badly, and then add stress on top of a system that is already depleted. The pelvic floor does particularly well with this: it responds to consistent light activity and regular release, and it does badly with long static loading and sustained tension.

One clarification on the original framing. "Avoid masturbation" and "do not push your body while it is recovering" are not the same instruction, and the second is the one with evidence behind it. Compulsive, edging-heavy, long sessions load the pelvic floor and aggravate prostatitis and CPPS symptoms. That is a mechanical problem, not a moral one. A regular, ordinary pattern is not what causes trouble.

If you are genuinely unwell — fever, night sweats, unexplained weight loss, pain that keeps waking you — then that is a medical question, not a lifestyle question, and no amount of abstaining is the answer to it.
