There is a small test I do with friends. I open a photograph on my phone, and ask them to look at it for one full minute without saying anything.

Most people cannot.

They swipe past in three seconds. The image becomes a fact they glanced at — like a headline, like weather. "Yes, I saw it." But they did not, really. They registered the rough shape of the scene and moved on.

## The minute problem

A photograph is a strange object. It freezes a single instant from a thousand angles of light, then asks you, the viewer, to spend time inside that frozen instant. Most images do not reward three seconds. They reward sixty.

> A photograph is not a fact. It is an opinion.

When you give a photo sixty seconds, you start noticing things: the slight blur in the corner, the way a shadow pools differently than you expected, the small accident of someone mid-blink in the background. These things are not decorations. They are the photo.

## What it changes

After a year of doing this test, I notice the photographs I linger on become richer over time. They keep giving. The ones I scroll past become flatter with each look — they lose substance instead of gaining it.

The minute is the unit. Spend it.
