The short answer: **release the pressure**. There is no medical reason to "tough it out," and there is no prostate harm from masturbating to relieve it. The discomfort you're feeling has a clinical name, is well understood, and resolves either way — but masturbation is faster and the surrounding evidence is reassuring, not alarming.

## What's actually happening

When arousal builds, blood pools in the penis, prostate, and epididymis. Ejaculation normally releases this pressure. Without it, the pooling produces the dull ache known medically as **epididymal hypertension** ("blue balls"). It is real, harmless, and self-resolving within minutes to a few hours. [Cleveland Clinic; WebMD]

## Does it cause prostatitis?

No reliable evidence links occasional prolonged erection without ejaculation to chronic prostatitis. The risk factors for CP/CPPS are pelvic floor dysfunction, central sensitization, and prior infection — not "holding it in" once or twice. [AUA 2025]

## Does it cause other damage?

No — with two important exceptions:

1. **Priapism** (erection lasting >4 hours, often painful, unrelated to arousal) is a urological emergency. It is not what blue balls is. Go to the ER.
2. **Repeated very long unresolved arousal** (hours, not minutes) with severe pain is uncommon and warrants a urology check.

## The actual downside of NOT releasing

The Harvard Health Professionals Follow-up Study (Rider et al., *European Urology* 2016) followed ~32,000 men for 18 years. Higher ejaculation frequency was associated with **lower** prostate cancer risk — about 20% lower for men reporting 21+ ejaculations per month versus 4–7. The mechanism is not certain, but the association is consistent. [Rider 2016, PMC5040619]

## The bottom line

For a single man with no partner, occasional arousal without ejaculation:

- **Masturbating to release** is medically safe, relieves the discomfort within minutes, and is consistent with the protective signal from the Harvard data.
- **Waiting it out** is also safe; the discomfort will pass in minutes to a few hours.
- **Neither** causes prostatitis or lasting harm.
- If the pain is severe, recurring without arousal, or accompanied by fever — see a urologist.

This is mechanical, not moral. The prostate and epididymis are not damaged by release; if anything, regular turnover is the better-supported pattern.
